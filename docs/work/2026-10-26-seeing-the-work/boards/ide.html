7 · Your own IDE: a session

Level 2, IDE access. Designed, not built: how a session is authorised is designed here (D3); the proof of concept is stretch. Agenthix bundles and hosts no IDE. A person's own desktop IDE connects to the live workspace for a session they start, under their own identity.

What the MVP leaves room for

Same relay and door, its own line. The relay carries streams, each opened with a ticket naming its purpose (R8), so an IDE session is one more purpose. It travels on a line of its own from the site, so downloads never slow typing, and a site with IDE access switched off refuses it outright. Nothing else in the MVP has to anticipate it. Why it will be asked for: Agenthix previews common kinds only, and people working on their own app's file types need their own tools.

The shape: the same fleet, one more kind of traffic

Designed, not built

1Started in hq

The person chooses Open in your IDE on a workspace (I1). hq checks they may change its files, and records a session: who, which workspace, when, how long (an hour, renewable).

2An access container

The site starts a container for the session, not an agent's container and not the site's machine: it mounts that one workspace and nothing else, with no secrets and no workflows (the workflows work). It ends when the session ends; the workspace stays.

3Through the relay

The IDE's SSH connection travels through the site's relay, like file bytes: a small helper on the person's computer (ProxyCommand) opens a connection to the relay with the session's ticket. Nothing on the site listens; the site dials the relay, as for files (D5).

4Ended anywhere

A session ends when it expires, when the person ends it, or when an admin or a change of membership ends it. hq tells the site on the control channel, and the site stops the container at once: unlike a file ticket, a session is revoked, not left to expire.
