The key fact

Two copies. Our transcript, in HQ's database, is what the person sees. The agent works from the runtime's own copy, which the runtime may compact without our transcript changing.

Claude (Agent SDK) reports compaction (compact_boundary: auto or manual, tokens before and after) and context usage (getContextUsage(): tokens, maximum, percentage).

OpenCode reports session.compacted or a compaction part, and tokens per reply against the model's limit.

Today the site forwards neither, and ignores OpenCode's context-overflow error. The build goes with context size and compaction.

  • adr-0076: a conversation whose harness state is gone is closed, never silently restarted.
  • The usage line's "in" figure is not the agent's memory: it adds up every model call in the run (The usage line).
  • The figure shown is always the runtime's, as of the last reply — never our own count: a bar filled to the share used, with "62% · 124k of 200k tokens", and a tick where the runtime summarises on its own (about 92%). Colour comes from the tokens alone: under 70% the accent, 70–89% the warning colour, 90% and over the danger colour; the "nearly full" notice appears from 90%.
  • The numbers: Claude — getContextUsage() returns totalTokens, maxTokens (the point it summarises at), rawMaxTokens (the model's window) and a percentage, and each result carries the model's contextWindow. OpenCode — each reply's tokens (input plus cache read is what the agent holds now) against the model's limit.context from its provider list.
  • 100 — the summary text: Claude gives it (compact_summary, and the PostCompact hook); OpenCode stores it as a message marked as a summary.
  • 101 — Summarise now: Claude — send /compact (reported as a manual compaction); OpenCode — the session's summarize call. Anyone who may send messages in the conversation may press it; it is disabled while the agent is working.
Why
  1. Nothing while there is room — a gauge on every conversation is noise.
  2. A quiet notice near the limit says what is about to happen, in the person's words: "the agent's memory", never "context".
  3. A marker where the runtime summarised, so an answer that forgot something has a visible reason, and the person knows they can still read it all.
  4. A closed conversation, not a silent restart: when the runtime no longer holds the conversation, the history stays, a notice says why, and a button starts a new conversation with the same agent — a lost memory is never a surprise (adr-0076).
  5. "Agent's memory" as a bar with its numbers in the conversation's header popup, for those who look.
  6. A figure and a bar let the person judge when to summarise; the colour warns before the runtime does it on its own.
  7. The summary is what the agent now knows — seeing it lets a person catch what was dropped and say it again.
  8. Summarising by choice, at a good moment, beats the runtime doing it mid-task.
