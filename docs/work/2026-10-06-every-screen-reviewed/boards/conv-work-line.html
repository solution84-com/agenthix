Why
  1. The order is kept. A run can go read, change, read, change; every step stays where it happened, and the thinking sits beside the steps it led to.
  2. A long list never pushes the reply away: at rest the work is one line, and the reply follows it directly.
  3. The folded line still says what changed ("created 3 tasks and changed 1", "edited 4, ran 9 commands"), counted from what happened, not from the agent's own words.
  4. Changes stand out in place: a mark, the verb in full weight, and a link to what changed.
  5. Long runs stay readable: same-kind steps in a row are one line, never merged across a change; past about 20 lines, the first and last steps with Show all.
  6. Plain words for each step ("Listed open tasks in AGX"), a command as the command itself; input, output and risk labels stay on the run's page.
  7. A stopped or failed run shows what it did — today that work vanishes.
